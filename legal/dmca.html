Sending a notice

A notice must be in writing and include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. The work you claim is infringed.
  3. The listing and URL you claim infringes, with enough detail for us to find it.
  4. Your contact details.
  5. A statement that you believe in good faith the use is not authorized by the owner, the owner's agent or the law.
  6. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that the notice is accurate and you are the owner or authorized to act for them.

What happens next

  1. Noodle disables the listing promptly and tells the developer, with a copy of the notice.
  2. The developer may send a counter-notice if they believe the removal was a mistake. A counter-notice must carry the same kind of signature, identification, contact details and a good-faith statement under penalty of perjury, and must consent to the courts that apply.
  3. Noodle sends the counter-notice to the complainant. If the complainant does not tell us within 10 to 14 business days that they have filed a court action, Noodle may restore the listing.
  4. Knowingly sending a false notice or counter-notice can carry legal liability.
